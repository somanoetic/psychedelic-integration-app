## A. Unresolved content tensions (need an editorial or sourcing decision)

### A1. Reconsolidation vs. prediction error — terminology overlap between articles #2 and #7  ⚠️ PRIORITY
- **The issue:** Article #2 ("Your Brain on Healing") originally claimed *memory reconsolidation* as a
  settled mechanism. The source check found reconsolidation is **NOT in** Barrett, Damasio, or Panksepp
  (searched — no matches). Those books support the broader idea of *prediction-error / mismatch learning*.
  The actual reconsolidation model (memory becomes labile when reactivated, updates within a window) is
  Ecker's territory — article #7.
- **What I did for now:** Reframed #2 §6 as "How Emotional Learning Updates" using prediction-error
  language, with a light pointer to #7 for the deeper mechanism. No reconsolidation citation in #2.
- **Still to resolve:**
  - Confirm the **division of labor** reads cleanly once #7 is drafted: #2 = "your emotional brain
    learns through mismatch" (general), #7 = "here is the specific reconsolidation mechanism" (Ecker).
    They must not contradict or sound like two competing claims.
  - Decide whether prediction error (Barrett's framing) and reconsolidation (Ecker's framing) should be
    explicitly named as *the same idea at different resolution*, or kept as two related-but-distinct lenses.
    Leaning toward: "two views of the same thing" — but wants the practitioner's call.
  - Source note: Ecker's *Unlocking the Emotional Brain* IS in-library; reconsolidation is well-sourced
    there. The gap is only that #2's source books don't cover it.

### A2. "How the Two Fit Together" (#2 §4) is a synthesis, not a single sourced claim
- **The issue:** Reconciling evolved circuits (Panksepp/Damasio) with constructed emotion (Barrett) is
  *our* bridge. Barrett's theory does explicitly hold both layers, but no single author states the joint
  framework outright.
- **What I did:** Drafted it, and added an in-text parenthetical flagging it as "a way of fitting the two
  views together, not a single claim every neuroscientist states outright."
- **To resolve:** Practitioner to confirm that hedge is enough, or decide to attribute the bridge more
  firmly to Barrett's integrative theory (which is the cleaner citation).

### A3. Attachment labels (#4) trace to Ainsworth/Main, who aren't in-library
- **The issue:** The four style *labels* (secure / anxious / avoidant / disorganized) originate with
  Ainsworth & Main. In-library books (Siegel IPNB-Clinical, Sensorimotor) name and use all four, so they're
  covered — but the originating research isn't a library source.
- **Plan (from outlines):** a light "(classic attachment research)" nod, no out-of-library citation.
- **To resolve at draft time:** confirm that nod is acceptable, vs. adding Ainsworth/Main to the source list
  even though the texts aren't imported.

## B. Omitted for lack of in-library evidence (claims we pulled OUT)

These were in the *existing* article or a tempting addition, but the books don't support them. Logged so we

### B1. Default Mode Network as the mechanism of ego dissolution  ⚠️ PRIORITY (source pending)
- **Removed claim:** "Psychedelics quiet the DMN, which is why the sense of self dissolves."
- **Why:** No in-library neuroscience source names the DMN as the mechanism. Seth notes resting-state
  networks "uncouple" but does NOT attribute self-dissolution to DMN disruption; Harris names no mechanism.
- **What I did:** Per practitioner's decision, wrote #2 §7 from **Seth/Harris only** — self-as-construction
  and ego-dissolution-as-possible — with an explicit in-text note that the brain mechanism "is still being
  worked out."
- **To unblock:** Practitioner is **adding a psychedelic-neuroscience book** (e.g. Carhart-Harris work,
  possibly Pollan). Once imported, we can decide whether to add a proper DMN section back. **Until then,
  no DMN mechanism claim ships.**

### B2. "Repetition literally reshapes your brain" (strong neuroplasticity mechanism)
- **Removed claim:** Hebbian "neurons that fire together wire together," repeated practice rewiring circuits.
- **Why:** Neither Barrett nor Damasio gives a detailed neuroplasticity mechanism. Barrett supports the
  softer "experience wires the microcircuits"; that's where I kept the prose.
- **To resolve (optional):** if we want the stronger, motivating "practice rewires your brain" line, add a
  dedicated neuroplasticity source (e.g. Doidge, *The Brain That Changes Itself*, or a Hebb/Kandel-based text).
  Low priority — the softer framing is honest and still hopeful.

### B3. "The amygdala is the fear center" (oversimplification)
- **Avoided claim:** amygdala = the seat of fear.
- **Why:** Barrett's case studies (patient SM) show the amygdala is necessary but not sufficient; multiple
  pathways produce fear. Kept the prose at "fast, ancient circuits react before the thinking brain" without
  pinning fear to one structure.
- **Status:** Resolved by avoidance; no source needed. Logged so it doesn't creep back in.

### B4. Specific brain-region mechanism claims in #2 generally
- The old article asserted insula/limbic/PFC connectivity changes under psychedelics as explanation for
  whole-body insight. That specific connectivity claim has no in-library source. Left out; the constructed-
  emotion + interoception framing covers the *experience* without the unsourced mechanism.
- **To unblock:** same psychedelic-neuroscience book as B1 would let us reintroduce a careful version.
